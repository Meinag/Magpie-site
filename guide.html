⚡Speed Cards — Racing Made Easier To Read

Gone are the days of looking through hundreds of columns. At Magpie we've made it as easy as we possibly can. The speed card puts what matters on one page for every race, and tells you what to look for in that race.

Every runner has a Magpie Speed Rating, built from GPS sectional data and weighted towards recent runs. Speed cards are not selections. They are the evidence, laid out so you can make up your own mind.

Reading a runner

Each runner shows the horse, its age, jockey, trainer and weight, then its Magpie Speed Rating. Open a runner to see its recent runs.

The tabs

  • Recent Figures — the last runs with date, track, distance, finishing position and speed figure. The going on the day is shown next to the distance: G good, GS good to soft, GF good to firm, S soft, H heavy, St standard.
  • Pace — how the race is likely to be run, and who it should suit.
  • Course / Class / Distance / Handicap — how the horse has done at today's course, level, trip and type of race.
  • Jockey / Trainer — how the people involved are performing.

What Matters Here

Every race has a short note on what to look for in that particular race: the going, the pace, whether the form is reliable. It's the quick read if you only have a minute.

How to use speed cards to your advantage

  • Check the going. Compare today's going with the going on a horse's best figures. A top figure on soft ground means less if it runs on firm today.
  • Look at the trend. Rising figures over recent runs are a better sign than one big number on its own.
  • Read the pace. If a race looks short of pace, front-runners can get an easy lead. If it's full of pace, hold-up horses get their chance.
  • Compare with the price. A strong rating at a bigger price is where the value can be.
  • Confirm or question a selection. If the card backs up a pick, good. If it doesn't, you now know why.

Leave yourself notes

Add a note to any horse you like. The next time it runs, your note is there waiting. Notes are saved on the device and browser you wrote them on, so they won't show on another phone or computer.

📊How It Works — Staking, P&L & The Website

The staking plan

Every selection is 1 point, level stakes. That means the same stake on every single pick — no chasing losses, no doubling up, no variable "confidence" stakes. One point, every time, win or lose.

Why level stakes? Because it's the only honest way to measure if a system actually works. If a tipster varies stakes, they can hide a losing system behind a couple of big-staked winners. Level stakes can't be fudged — the profit is real or it isn't.

A "point" is whatever you choose it to be. If £10 = 1 point, a +50pt week = +£500. If £2 = 1 point, it's +£100. Scale it to your own bankroll — never bet more than you're comfortable losing.

How the P&L is calculated

Profit/loss is worked out at advised price — the official price every horse is returned at when the race goes off.

  • A winner returns its advised price minus the stake.
  • A loser loses the 1pt stake → −1 point.
Example: a horse wins at 5.0 advised → profit = 5.0 − 1 = +4 points.

Each day we add up every selection: winners pay out at their advised price, losers cost 1pt each. The total is the day's P&L. Add the days together = the running record.

Why the advised price? Because it's independently verifiable — nobody can claim a fake price. Every result is settled at the real number the market returned. Wins AND losers are all counted — no cherry-picking the good days.

📖How To Use The Tips

Rank 1 vs Rank 2

Each race has two selections:

  • Rank 1 = the model's strongest pick. Higher strike rate (wins more often), usually shorter odds.
  • Rank 2 = the value angle. Wins less often but at bigger prices — and over time it's actually been the bigger earner.

You can back both, or just one. Backing both per race covers more winners but costs more in stakes. Backing Rank 1 only is tighter and steadier. Your choice — the record tracks both separately so you can see how each performs.

Win or each-way?

  • Under 5/1 (6.0): 1 point to win — at short prices the place part isn't worth much.
  • 6.0 (5/1) and up: back each-way (0.5pt each-way) — you get paid if the horse just places, which cushions the longer-odds picks.

The record follows the staking rule above — win under 6.0, each-way at 6.0 and bigger — which suits the longer-priced Rank 2 value picks especially.

Staking — keep it simple

  • Under 5/1 (6.0): 1pt win. 6.0+: 0.5pt each-way. Same rule every time.
  • Set your point size to your bankroll. A common rule: 1 point = 1–2% of your total betting bank. So a £200 bank → £2–£4 a point. That way a losing run never wipes you out.
  • Never chase. Losing days happen — even a profitable system has them. Flat stakes through the downswings is how the edge plays out.
  • Only bet what you can afford to lose. This is entertainment with an edge, not income.

Timing your bets

Back every selection at advised price. Take the advised price shown, ideally the night before or early — that is the price our record settles at.

The prices shown in each post are a current-market guide only. Prices drift and steam through the day, so the morning figure is rarely the final one. The advised price is the real, takeable price — and it's exactly what our record settles at. Grabbing the advised price early is the point - you beat the closing market.

Judge it over weeks, not days

One day means little — good or bad. The edge shows over a proper sample. Follow the running record on the site and judge the system over weeks. That's how value betting works.
